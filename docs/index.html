rill

The plain-English cover page for this repo.

Chosen sources, finite reading

The web without the feed machine.

Rill is a small personal reader for blogs, newsletters, and publications you choose yourself. It brings new posts into one quiet place and stops there.

Stupid simple version

Rill lets you subscribe to RSS, Atom, and JSON feeds, then read the newest posts in order. No ranking. No recommendations. No infinite scroll. You read what arrived, save what matters, search locally, and leave.

What you do with it

01

Add sources

Paste a site or feed URL. Rill finds RSS, Atom, or JSON Feed when the site exposes one.

02

Open Today

See what arrived since your last visit. The list has an end, so reading can be done.

03

Read cleanly

Open one article at a time in a quiet reader. Mark it read, archive it, or save it for later.

04

Keep control

Search your local copy, manage subscriptions, and import or export OPML so the list stays yours.

Why it is private by design

What stays on your device

  • Your reading state lives in IndexedDB in the browser.
  • Search is local full-text search, not a server query.
  • Offline changes queue locally, then sync when the app can reach the Worker.

What the server does

  • A Cloudflare Worker fetches feeds and serves the API.
  • D1 stores feed and entry state so refreshes can dedupe cleanly.
  • Queues and Cron refresh feeds on a schedule with conditional HTTP.

What it refuses to become

No algorithmic layer

Rill does not rank, recommend, cluster, summarize, or try to keep you around.

No surveillance feel

No analytics SDKs, no third-party scripts, no external fonts, and a privacy lint pass that checks the built output.

No fake media

Remote images are hidden by default. If you load one, it goes through the server-side image proxy.

No lock-in

Your subscriptions can move with you through OPML import and export.

Built from this repo's README, docs, and package scripts. The project shape is simple: a local-first React PWA, a Cloudflare Worker, D1, Queues, Cron, and strict privacy checks.
